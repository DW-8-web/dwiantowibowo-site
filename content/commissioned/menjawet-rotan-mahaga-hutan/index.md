*“Menjawet Rotan, Mahaga Hutan”* (*Weaving Rattan, Protecting Forests*) is a short documentary capturing the living indigenous heritage of the Dayak people and women's weaving collectives in Katingan Regency, Central Kalimantan.

In the Dayak Ngaju language, *menjawet* signifies the time-honored craft of weaving rattan, while *mahaga* embodies the shared commitment of guardianship and stewardship over nature. Because natural rattan (*uwei*) is a climbing vine that depends fundamentally on mature canopy trees to thrive, the survival of artisanal weaving traditions is inextricably linked to the protection of standing primary rainforests. Nurturing and harvesting wild rattan not only sustains rural household economies for women artisans, but also creates a resilient community defense against deforestation and extractive land conversions.

The documentary traces the collective journey of women weavers, traditional rattan harvesters, and indigenous communities in Katingan as they organize community associations to manage non-timber forest products justly, sustainably, and with dignity.
